SERVICE ANIMALS. A PUBLIC POLICY QUESTION.

Their work matters.
So does their
protection.

A service animal’s protection should reflect the assistance it provides, not depend solely on who trained it.

Magda’s Law proposes updating the Criminal Code so that legitimate service animals recognized under provincial or territorial law are not excluded solely because they were trained outside a professional institution.

01 / UNDERSTAND

The same animal.
The same assistance.
A different legal test.

Magda’s Law, in plain language.

An animal can be recognized as a service animal in daily life and still fall outside the Criminal Code’s special service-animal protection.

The issue is how the law decides which animals qualify. Magda’s Law asks Parliament to address the gap between provincial or territorial recognition and the federal definition.

What does the federal law say?

Section 445.01 of the Criminal Code creates a specific offence for wilfully killing or injuring a service animal without lawful excuse. Its definition requires an animal needed for disability-related assistance to have written certification of training by a professional service-animal institution.

An animal’s actual assistance, or recognition under another law, does not by itself satisfy that institutional-training requirement. Read section 445.01.

Where does the mismatch arise?

Ontario recognizes service animals under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act using a different test from the Criminal Code. For access to goods, services and facilities covered by these provisions of the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, an animal can qualify through specified visual indicators or documentation from a listed regulated health professional confirming a disability-related need. The same institutional-training requirement does not apply. Read the regulation under the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act, section 80.45(4).

That means an owner-trained animal may be recognized under these provisions of the Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act while failing the separate federal test. The animal, the person’s disability and the assistance being provided have not changed. The legal definition has.

Ontario illustrates the problem. Provincial and territorial rules differ; this proposal seeks a federal response to that difference in recognition.

Why does it matter in practice?

When a service animal is injured, the person who relies on it may lose essential assistance with everyday life. During recovery, that person may also take on the physical work of caring for the animal that ordinarily assists them.

The proposal’s concern is that an institutional-training requirement can prevent the specific service-animal offence from applying even when that service relationship is real. This can affect the legal options considered after an attack, before a case ever reaches a courtroom.

What is Magda’s Law asking for?

Amend the definition in section 445.01(4) so that a legitimate service animal recognized under the laws of a Canadian province or territory is not excluded solely because it was not trained by a professional service-animal institution.

The aim is continuity of recognition: the federal protection should account for a service relationship already recognized under applicable provincial or territorial law.

Retaining an established service-animal status under federal law

The law of the province or territory where the handler lives determines when an animal qualifies as a service animal. Recognition under the applicable law is grounded in a disability related need and the animal’s demonstrated ability to assist safely and reliably. It does not mean the animal has gone without training. Magda’s Law proposes that an animal recognized under those laws be eligible for the Criminal Code’s service-animal protection. Who provided its training should not erase a status it has already established under applicable law.

Would every injury lead to a criminal conviction?

No. Changing the definition would not establish guilt. The prosecution would still have to prove the offence. Other animal-cruelty provisions may also apply where their requirements are met; exclusion from section 445.01 does not mean an animal has no legal protection. Read section 445.1.

02 / THE LEGISLATIVE PROPOSAL

Carry recognition
into protection.

Magda’s Law builds on the service-animal protection introduced through Quanto’s Law. It asks Parliament to change who qualifies under that protection.

Read the 2015 legislation

PROPOSAL IN DEVELOPMENT

This is a plain-language account of the proposed approach. The full legislative document will be added after finalization and presentation to the local Member of Parliament.

01

The provision

The service-animal definition in Criminal Code section 445.01(4).

02

The barrier

Institutional training can determine eligibility even where provincial or territorial law recognizes the animal’s service role.

03

The requested change

Recognize qualifying service animals without excluding them solely because they were trained outside a professional institution.

04

The intended result

A federal definition that better reflects the service relationships people with disabilities rely on.

03 / ABOUT MAGDA’S LAW

A real animal.
A working partner.
A reason to begin.

Magda is the origin and namesake of this proposal.

She is a working service animal who experienced an attack, underwent a long recovery and returned to work.

Her experience prompted this initiative. Her story is shared here with respect for her working role, strength and perseverance.

The case for an amendment must rest on the legislative wording, its rationale and supporting evidence. Readers should be able to evaluate that case on its own merits.
